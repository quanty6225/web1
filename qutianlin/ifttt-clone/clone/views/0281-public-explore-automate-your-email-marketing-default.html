5 ways to automate your email marketing with IFTTT

By The IFTTT Team

Published August 4, 2026

Updated September 16, 2026

Somewhere in your stack, there's a MailerLite account that knows exactly who opened your last campaign. There's a Kit account (still catching up on the fact that it used to be called ConvertKit) that knows who just tagged themselves as "interested in the pro plan." There's a Constant Contact list that knows which of your webinar attendees never got a follow-up. All of that information is sitting right there. It just never leaves the platform that collected it.

That's the actual problem with email marketing in 2026. It was never really about writing the email. It's about everything that happens the second before and the second after: the signup that should trigger something, the open that should mean something, the unsubscribe that should be a signal instead of a shrug. Most of that information just sits in a dashboard, waiting for someone to log in and check.

That's where IFTTT comes in. IFTTT is an automation platform that connects over 1,000 apps and services, letting one thing trigger another without anyone in the middle doing it by hand.

Here are five ways to get your email platform talking to the rest of your stack, or chat with IFTTT and describe the automation you're picturing instead.

1. Turn signups into subscribers automatically

A signup is a small, easy-to-miss event. Someone fills out a form, RSVPs to something, or shows up in a feed you follow, and that's usually the last anyone thinks about it until the next campaign goes out. But that moment deserves to travel further than the platform it landed in, no matter which email tool you're running or where the signup actually happened.

2. Tag and segment people the moment they show interest

For creators, the goal was never a list. It was an audience, which is a different thing entirely. An audience gets tagged, segmented, and treated like the specific person they are instead of a row in a spreadsheet, the second they show you what they care about instead of three campaigns later.

Kit and Campaign Monitor both make tagging part of the workflow itself, Constant Contact can sort people the moment a lead comes in, and Customer.io take it a step further by segmenting people based on what they actually do.

3. Know who's actually opening, clicking, and engaging

Sending the email is the easy part. Knowing whether anyone cared is where most teams go quiet, because open, click, and signup data tends to live inside the platform that generated it and nowhere else.

Whether that's an open in MailerLite, a send in Campaign Monitor, a new subscriber in Kit, a new contact in Constant Contact, or a campaign summary in Mailchimp, a notification the moment something happens puts that signal somewhere you'll actually see it, instead of behind a login.

4. Keep a running, synced record of every subscriber

Most teams find out their subscriber count the hard way: by logging into four different dashboards and adding the numbers up themselves. A running log that updates on its own means the record is always current, no matter which platform someone actually signed up through, and it makes it easier to spot the accounts worth double-checking.

5. Turn real-world bookings and payments into subscribers

Not every subscriber starts with a newsletter signup. Some of them booked a call. Some of them paid for something. The people who show up for an appointment or complete a purchase are already telling you they're interested, and that interest shouldn't have to be re-entered by hand into your email platform.

The rest of your stack is probably already covered

None of this requires ripping out your current email platform or convincing your team to learn a new tool. It just means the moment someone subscribes, opens, clicks, or walks away doesn't have to end at the login screen. If your platform's already connected to IFTTT, the Applets above are waiting. If it's not, there's a decent chance it's one of the 1,000+ services IFTTT already talks to, right alongside your CRM, your forms, your scheduling app, and your spreadsheets.

You can build any of this from scratch, or chat with IFTTT and describe what you're trying to automate in plain language instead.
