How to add a delay to an Applet

Getting started

After a Trigger occurs, an Applet’s Action will usually run within a few seconds. If you’d prefer to have the Action run at a later time, you can add a Delay between your Trigger and Actions, giving you more flexibility in how your Applet runs.

Delays are useful for sending follow-up notifications, scheduling social media posts or emails, or anytime you want to wait before an Action runs.

How long can I delay an Action?

The Delay feature lets you choose a delay of 15 seconds to 3 hours between the Trigger and Action. You can add a Delay by including it in your prompt to IFTTT Chat when creating an Applet, or add one directly using the Classic Applet Composer on ifttt.com/create.

When does the delay occur?

The Delay occurs after the Applet's Trigger, Query, and filter code, and before the Action. To add a Delay between Actions, see our guide here. 

Adding a delay using IFTTT Chat

When creating an Applet with IFTTT Chat, include the desired delay in your prompt. For example, you could ask:

Any time I post on my Facebook Page, cross post it to Twitter but include a 5 minute delay.

IFTTT Chat will add the Delay to your Applet based on your request.

You can also ask IFTTT Chat to add a Delay to an existing Applet. Just describe the Applet you want to modify and specify how long you want the delay to be.

Adding a delay using IFTTT the Classic Composer

  1. Navigate to the Applet composer via ifttt.com/create.
  2. Add a Trigger of your choice.
  3. Click the "plus (+) sign" the click Add to the right of 'Delay'.

     

  4. Set the length of time you want the delay to occur between the Trigger and the Action.
     


     

  5. Add at least one Action to your Applet
  6. Press Continue, then press Finish to save your Applet

Examples of using a delay

Smart Home Delays:

Delays can be very useful for automating your smart home devices to turn them on and then off after a set amount of time. For example, if you’d like to turn on a light, then turn it off five minutes later this can be done using Delays and the Webhooks service.

Set up an Applet with a Trigger of your choice paired with an Action to turn on your smart light bulb and another Action to Make a web request. Configure a second Applet with the Receive a web request Trigger, include a delay of five minutes, and an Action to turn off your bulb.

Email Automation:

If you are automating emails or text messages to potential customers with IFTTT, you may want to put a 30 second or 5 minute delay between when the customer fills out the lead form, and you follow up with an automated message. You can also build an entire email drip flow using a few delay steps and emails.
