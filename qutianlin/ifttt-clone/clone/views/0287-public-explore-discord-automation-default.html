Automate your Discord server with IFTTT

By The IFTTT Team

Published May 1, 2026

Updated September 21, 2026

You go live on Twitch on a Tuesday afternoon. Nobody in your Discord finds out until you post about it yourself, twenty minutes in, after the stream's already half over.

Same story with a new YouTube upload, or a commit landing in your repo. None of it reaches the server unless a person carries it there by hand.

IFTTT carries it there instead. Connect Discord to wherever the activity is actually happening, and it shows up the moment it does, not whenever someone remembers to paste the link.

Skip the bot entirely

You look up how to get GitHub commits posting into your dev channel, and the first result wants you to rent a server, write some JavaScript, and keep a bot running yourself. If it crashes at 2AM, so does your automation.

Connecting Discord to IFTTT skips that setup. Pick a trigger from another app, authorize Discord once, and IFTTT posts the message for you from there on.

Go live, get seen

Six hours after you post something isn't the same as the moment you post it. A video that dropped this morning already lost half its window. A stream that just started on Twitch is the whole reason people show up in the first place.

Upload a new video, and it lands in your server the second YouTube confirms it's public. Go live, and IFTTT sends the announcement before your first viewer finds the stream on their own. Post something new on Reddit, and the same thing happens there too, without you copying the link over yourself.

Bring your dev work into Discord

A dev team split between GitHub and Discord loses time in the gap between the two. A commit lands, and the server doesn't know until someone mentions it in passing.

Push a commit, and it posts to your dev channel automatically. Something else happens in the repo, a new issue, a fresh pull request, and Discord catches that too. Flip it around and pull activity out of Discord itself: log a new message to a Google Sheets row, or post a new spreadsheet entry straight back into the channel instead of leaving it buried where nobody opens it.

Bring the outside world in

A community built around a shared interest usually cares about more than whatever's happening inside the server itself. A new post from an RSS feed you follow, a tweet from the right account, a thread from a subreddit your members actually read, all of it matters more in the moment than a week later when someone happens to stumble on it.

Point IFTTT at any RSS feed, and new posts land in Discord and back themselves up to Google Drive at the same time. Follow an account on X, and every new post shows up in your server as it happens. Point one feed at both Discord and Reddit, and the same story reaches both audiences without you writing it twice.

Your repo, now talking to your server

Rust doesn't pause the game to tell your teammates something's wrong. A smart alarm goes off because someone's cutting through your wall, and if nobody's staring at their own screen right then, the raid's already happening before anyone in the clan notices.

Connect Rust to Discord, and a triggered alarm posts straight to your server the second it happens, keyword filter and all if you only want to hear about the alarms that matter. Get killed, and the same channel finds out too, so your clan knows what happened even if you're not around to type it yourself. And for something a little less life-or-death, Steam achievements can post to Discord automatically, a small win worth sharing without you stopping mid-game to screenshot it.

Let your house post in Discord too

A smart home doesn't need to stay siloed in its own app. A plug turns on, motion trips a sensor, the rain gauge hits a threshold, and none of it means anything to anyone unless it shows up somewhere people actually look.

Point a Govee device at Discord, and a status change posts to your channel the moment it happens. An eWeLink sensor picks up motion, and the same thing. Set a rainfall or outdoor temperature threshold, and Discord becomes the place your household (or your smart-home-obsessed server) finds out first, instead of a phone notification nobody else sees.
