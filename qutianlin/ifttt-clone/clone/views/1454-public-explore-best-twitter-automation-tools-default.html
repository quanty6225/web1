Every way to automate X (Twitter) with IFTTT

By The IFTTT Team

Published August 18, 2026

Updated September 16, 2026

Most people who search for Twitter automation are trying to solve one of three problems: getting content onto X without manually re-posting it everywhere, keeping a consistent posting schedule without sitting at a keyboard at a set time every day, or keeping track of hashtags and mentions without refreshing search results all day. This page is a working reference for all three, organized by which problem you're actually trying to solve, using real, published Applets on X (Twitter).

One thing worth saying up front, since it comes up in search a lot: none of this is about buying followers or running a bot that fakes engagement. Managing multiple social accounts and keeping a posting schedule consistent is a real, common problem, and it's the one this page actually solves.

How X (Twitter) automation works on IFTTT

X Applets on IFTTT fall into two directions. The first posts to X automatically, triggered by something happening on another service (a new blog post, a new video, a scheduled time) or by X itself (a new tweet you post, a hashtag you use). The second direction watches X and reacts elsewhere, useful for tracking mentions, hashtags, or search terms without living in the app. Nothing here requires the X API directly. IFTTT holds that connection for you once you link your account.

Cross-post content from everywhere else into X

If you publish somewhere else first (a blog, a YouTube channel, another social account), these Applets keep X updated automatically instead of adding a manual repost to your checklist every time.

Post new RSS feed items to X (Twitter): turns any RSS Feed into an auto-posting X account, works for a blog, a podcast, or a newsletter archive.

Share new YouTube uploads to X (Twitter): tweets every new public YouTube upload automatically.

Share new WordPress posts on X (Twitter): posts a tweet the moment a new WordPress article goes live.

Share new Blogger posts to X (Twitter): the same pattern for Blogger.

Share new Tumblr posts to X (Twitter): tweets your public Tumblr posts with title and link.

Share your Instagram videos automatically on X (Twitter): cross-posts new Instagram videos with caption and thumbnail attached.

Post your Facebook status to X (Twitter): mirrors plain-text Facebook status updates to X.

Share new Facebook Page link posts to X (Twitter): the business-page version, for link posts from a Facebook Pages account.

Schedules and recurring posts

For accounts that run on a posting rhythm rather than reacting to new content elsewhere, these Applets post on a timer using the Date & Time service.

Post a daily tweet on X (Twitter): fires once a day at a time you set, good for a daily quote, tip, or reminder account.

Post scheduled X tweets on chosen weekdays: the weekday-only version, useful for business accounts that don't want weekend posts.

Post a scheduled tweet every Saturday on X (Twitter): a weekly cadence for something like a roundup or a recurring feature.

Post hourly updates to X (Twitter): a much higher frequency option, best reserved for accounts built specifically around constant updates.

Monitor hashtags, mentions, and searches

This is the listening half of Twitter automation: instead of checking the app for what people are saying, these Applets bring the relevant tweets to you.

Get notified of new X (Twitter) search results: a Notifications alert for any saved search term, useful for tracking a brand name, product, or topic.

Get an IFTTT notification for new X (Twitter) mentions: flags every time someone mentions your account.

Get IFTTT notification for new X hashtag tweets: the hashtag-specific version, good for live events or campaign tracking.

Log new X hashtag mentions to Google Sheets: builds a running spreadsheet of every tweet matching a hashtag, useful for reporting on a campaign after the fact instead of just reacting in the moment.

Share new X hashtag tweets to a Slack channel: routes matching tweets into Slack so a team can monitor mentions together instead of one person checking an app.

Email me new X (Twitter) search results: the lowest-effort version of the same idea, straight to your inbox.

Archive and back up your own tweets

A quieter use case: keeping your own posting history somewhere more permanent or more searchable than X's own timeline.

Save liked X (Twitter) posts to Google Docs: every tweet you like gets appended to a running document, a simple way to build a reference library out of things you found worth saving.

Save your new X (Twitter) posts to Day One journal: backs up your own tweets into a Day One journal entry, which doubles as a personal archive if you ever needed to export your posting history.

Save X tweets with a hashtag to Google Docs: the hashtag-filtered version, good for pulling together everything you've posted about a specific project or event.

Save liked tweets from X to Notion: if you already keep research and reading material in Notion.so, liked tweets can land there instead of Google Docs.
