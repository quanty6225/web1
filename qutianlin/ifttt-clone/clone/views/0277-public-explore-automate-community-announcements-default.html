Automate your community announcements across Discord, X, and newsletter

By The IFTTT Team

Published August 6, 2026

Updated September 17, 2026

It's 9PM and you just posted an update in your Discord server. Now you have to remember to tweet it. And put it in the newsletter. And drop it in Telegram for the members who never check Discord. By the time you've retyped the same three sentences into four different text boxes, you've spent more time formatting the announcement than you did writing it in the first place.

If you run a community, you already know this cycle. One idea, five destinations, and you're the one manually carrying it between them.

The good news is you don't need a unified social media suite or a full-time social manager to fix this. IFTTT can watch the places your announcements start (Discord, Telegram, X, a spreadsheet) and push them out to the places your members actually are. If you don't already have an account, you can sign up for IFTTT free in about a minute, you'll need it to build any of the Applets below.

How the pipeline works with IFTTT

Community content usually starts in one of two places: a live conversation (Discord), (Telegram) or a planned post (Substack), a content calendar). With IFTTT sitting between those starting points and the rest of your channels, it needs to reach the other places your members use without a person carrying it there by hand.

The pipeline breaks into two paths, both built entirely with IFTTT Applets:

Capture and route a new idea: Discord → Google Sheets → Webhook

Cross-post something already published: Substack, X, Telegram, or Threads → Buffer, Discord, Threads, or X

You don't need to build both at once. Start with whichever path matches your current bottleneck, and add the other later. Here's how to build it, step by step:

Step 1: Capture ideas from Discord before they're gone

Good announcement ideas get born in the middle of a Discord conversation, then disappear under fifty messages an hour later. This Applet gives them a place to land.

Set a designated channel, maybe #content-ideas or #announcements-drafts, and every new message posted there gets appended as a row to a Google Sheet automatically.

Now instead of hoping someone remembers to screenshot the good idea, it's already sitting in a backlog, ready for whoever handles your content calendar.

    Log Discord ideas to social media backlog
    Discord

Step 2: Turn that backlog into outbound requests

Once ideas are landing in a spreadsheet, the next step is getting them out to wherever your team actually schedules and publishes. This Applet fires whenever a new row hits your planning sheet and sends the row's contents to a webhook URL, so your scheduler, CMS, or internal tool can pick it up.

This is the connective tissue between "someone had an idea in Discord" and "that idea is now a scheduled post," without a person manually retyping it into three systems along the way.

    Send drafts to webhook to automate social posts
    Google

Step 3: Get newsletter posts into your social queue

If your newsletter lives on Substack, every new post can queue itself into Buffer the moment it goes live. No separate reminder to "also post about this on social."

Buffer handles the actual scheduling from there, publishing to whichever channel you've queued it for on its normal cadence.

Step 4: Keep Discord in the loop on campaign updates

Not every announcement should be routed into a spreadsheet first. Sometimes you just want a live campaign tweet to show up where your community actually spends its time. Tag your campaign tweets with a specific hashtag, and this Applet drops them straight into your Discord announcement channel.

Moderators and members see the update the moment it posts, without anyone pasting a link into the channel manually.

    Publish campaign tweets to Discord for builders

Step 5: Stretch a Telegram post across Threads

If your community spans both Telegram and Threads, this Applet keeps them in sync. A new post in your Telegram channel gets republished as a text post on Threads automatically. Same announcement, two audiences, one write-up.

    Republish Telegram to Threads for communities
    Telegram

Step 6: Close the loop from Threads to X

And to round out the chain, anything you post fresh on Threads can cross-post to X the moment it publishes.

Put steps 5 and 6 together and a single post can travel from Telegram to Threads to X without you touching either platform after the first one.

Automate your community's announcements in minutes

None of the Applets in this pipeline require the others to work. But stacked together, they cover most of what a community manager juggles by hand: catching ideas before they're lost, getting them into a real publishing workflow, and making sure a post on one platform doesn't die there.

Start with whichever piece matches your actual bottleneck right now. If ideas keep getting lost in Discord, start with Step 1. If your newsletter and social feel disconnected, start with Step 3. Set the pipeline up once with IFTTT, and every announcement that follows moves through it on its own.

FAQs

Do I need to set up all six of these Applets?

No. Each one solves a specific handoff. Most community managers start with one or two, usually the capture step (Discord to spreadsheet) or the cross-post step (Telegram to Threads, or Threads to X), and add more as the workload grows.

What if my team doesn't use Google Sheets for planning?

The Discord-to-backlog and backlog-to-webhook Applets both use Google Sheets as the middle step, but the webhook action can point to almost any tool with an inbound API, so the sheet is really just a lightweight staging area. If your team already plans in a different tool, you can often skip the sheet and trigger a webhook Applet directly from that tool's own IFTTT integration, if one exists.

Can I trigger this from a form instead of Discord?

Yes. Google Forms and Google Sheets pair naturally (a form submission adds a row to a sheet), so if your team prefers submitting announcement ideas through a form rather than a Discord channel, you can swap the trigger and keep the same webhook step downstream.

Will this work if my community spans more than the platforms listed here?

The same pattern extends to most services IFTTT supports. If you use Slack, Mastodon, or another platform instead of one shown here, look for the equivalent trigger or action on that service's IFTTT page and slot it into the same chain.

Does this replace a dedicated social media management tool?

For most one-to-three-person community teams, this pipeline covers the core repetitive handoffs: capture, log, and cross-post. If you're managing dozens of channels or need detailed analytics across platforms, a dedicated social suite will still outperform a chain of Applets, but you can run both together, using IFTTT for the connective tissue a bigger tool doesn't cover natively.
