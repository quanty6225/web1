Best smart locks for vacation rentals: Airbnb-friendly picks for remote hosts

By The IFTTT Team

Published July 31, 2026

Updated September 18, 2026

Self-check-in has shifted from a nice bonus to a baseline expectation for many guests. For remote hosts, a smart lock is what makes that possible without key handoffs, lockboxes, or late-night coordination.

But the lock itself is only half the decision. The bigger win is everything you can automate around it: guest-ready lighting, thermostat prep before arrival, notifications when check-in is coming up, and backup workflows if something needs attention while you are off-site.

That is where IFTTT becomes useful. Once you have the right lock in place, you can connect the rest of the stay around it and make the property feel more reliable for guests and less hands-on for you. If you want a broader framework for that setup, How to create a smart home manager with IFTTT is a strong next step.

Here are the smart locks that make the most sense for vacation rentals in 2026, depending on the kind of property you run.

How IFTTT fits into a remote-host setup

A smart lock solves entry. IFTTT helps you automate what happens around entry.

For a vacation rental, that can mean:

  • - Turning on entry lights before guest arrival setting the thermostat to a comfortable range before check-in.
  • - Sending yourself or a co-host reminders around arrival windows triggering backup notifications if you need to check the property status remotely.
  • - Tying your lock choice into a broader smart home routine so the rental feels ready before guests walk in.

That matters because the best lock is not just the one that opens the door. It is the one that works cleanly inside the hosting workflow you want to run.

Best overall: Schlage Encode Plus

The Schlage Encode Plus is the safest default recommendation for most remote hosts. It is the pick for someone who wants a strong blend of reliability, remote access, and physical security without having to build around extra hubs or workarounds.

Its biggest advantage is confidence. If you manage a property from a distance and want a lock that feels sturdy, dependable, and guest-friendly, Schlage is an easy choice. The Grade 1 security rating is worth mentioning here because it actually changes the buying decision for hosts who care about protecting a property between stays, not just making check-in easier.

The tradeoff is price. You are paying more for peace of mind, and some hosts may find Yale a little more flexible if they are standardizing across several units.

Choose this if: you want the strongest all-around recommendation and care as much about property security as guest convenience.

What automations can do: once this lock is in place, the useful IFTTT layer is everything around arrival and turnover — lights on before check-in, temperature adjusted ahead of time, and simple remote-host alerts so you are not manually checking the same things before every stay.

Most popular and future-proof: Yale Assure Lock 2

The Yale Assure Lock 2 is one of the safest picks for hosts who want flexibility over time. It is especially appealing if you manage more than one property, expect your setup to evolve, or want a lock that fits neatly into a broader smart home system.

What makes Yale stand out is how easy it is to imagine scaling with it. If you are running multiple units, or think you may add more, Yale feels like the practical operator's choice: clean guest-code handling, good ecosystem fit, and less sense that you are painting yourself into a corner.

The tradeoff is that it is not really the "buy it for maximum physical security" recommendation in the same way Schlage is. It is the better choice when flexibility, compatibility, and portfolio consistency matter more.

Choose this if: you want a strong long-term option for one property today and a cleaner standard across multiple properties tomorrow.

What automations can do: Yale is a natural fit for layered guest-arrival workflows in IFTTT, including prepping lights, climate, and remote notifications around stay windows so you are managing the whole guest-ready experience, not just the code on the door.

Best for HOAs and rental arbitrage: August Wi-Fi Smart Lock

August is the smart workaround for hosts who cannot swap exterior hardware. If you are in a condo, dealing with HOA restrictions, running a rental arbitrage setup, or working with a door that cannot easily be changed, this is the option that keeps you in the game.

Its biggest value is that it lets you upgrade access without making your door look different from the outside. That is not a small thing. For the right host, it is the difference between being able to offer self-check-in at all and having to stick with keys or lockboxes.

The tradeoff is guest experience. August works best when you add the keypad accessory, because relying too much on app-based entry is not what most hosts want for guests who just want a code and a smooth arrival.

Choose this if: exterior hardware restrictions matter more than having the cleanest all-in-one lock setup.

What automations can do: this is where IFTTT helps offset the compromise. If the hardware setup is a little more constrained, you can still make the stay feel polished by automating the surrounding experience — entry lights, climate prep, and simple host-side reminders or alerts around check-in.

August connects to IFTTT directly, so it's one of the easier locks on this list to wire into a turnover routine — welcome lighting on unlock, a security handoff when the door is secured, and a running log of lock activity for your own records.

Mid-range pick without the premium price: Kwikset Halo/Halo Touch

The Kwikset Halo is the practical middle-ground pick. It is for hosts who want built-in Wi-Fi and a straightforward remote-access setup, but do not want to pay Schlage or Yale prices for every unit.

That makes it appealing for owners with one or two properties who care more about affordability and simplicity than having the most polished ecosystem or the most advanced host workflow. It does the core job well enough without asking you to overthink the hardware.

The main downside is that it does not give Airbnb hosts the same hands-off code flow as Schlage, Yale, or August. Manual code management is still manageable at small scale, but it becomes annoying quickly if you are trying to run a truly low-touch operation.

Choose this if: you want a decent remote-hosting setup at a lower cost and do not mind doing a little more guest-code admin yourself.

What automations can do: IFTTT is especially helpful here because it can reduce some of the other repetitive host work even when the code workflow is still manual — pre-arrival prep, lighting, climate adjustments, and reminders around guest turnover still create time savings.

Kwikset's own IFTTT service can also pick up some of the slack the native app leaves behind — scheduling locks around checkout, or giving yourself a one-tap remote unlock for a maintenance visit without touching the physical keypad.

Best for spotty or no Wi-Fi: igloohome Smart Deadbolt

igloohome is the right recommendation when connectivity is the real problem. If you manage a cabin, rural property, or any rental where internet reliability is shaky, this is the lock that makes the most sense because it is built around that reality instead of pretending your Wi-Fi is better than it is.

That is what makes it so useful for certain properties. In a city condo with stable internet, there are smoother options. In a place where signal drops are part of normal life, reliability matters more than elegance, and igloohome is solving the problem that actually matters.

The tradeoff is that it is not the cleanest fit for hosts who want the most seamless Airbnb-native workflow. It wins because it works in hard conditions, not because it has the nicest software story.

Choose this if: your property is remote enough that dependable access matters more than fancy integration.

What automations can do: even if the lock itself is not the most connected option on the list, IFTTT can still support the rest of the guest-ready environment — arrival lighting, temperature prep, and host-side routines that make the property feel managed even when the internet is imperfect.

Best budget option: Wyze Lock Bolt

The Wyze Lock Bolt is the budget pick for hosts who want a real upgrade from keys without spending much. For a nearby property, spare unit, or occasional rental where you can step in if needed, it is a sensible low-cost option.

The important thing is knowing what you are buying. This is not the lock for a fully remote portfolio. It is the lock for a host who is close enough to handle the edge cases, and who is comfortable trading convenience at scale for a much lower upfront cost.

That tradeoff is what keeps it from being a stronger recommendation for most Airbnb-style setups. Cheap hardware is only cheap until it creates hands-on work you were trying to avoid in the first place.

Choose this if: cost is your top priority and the property is close enough that you can be hands-on when necessary.

What automations can do: with a lock like this, IFTTT becomes more about reducing the rest of the hosting friction — arrival reminders, guest-ready lighting, climate prep, and simple property-status routines — rather than building a truly set-it-and-forget-it remote access system.

Since the Wyze ecosystem is where this lock lives, the most useful applets are the ones that stitch it into your existing Wyze cams and bulbs — welcoming light at sunset, camera notifications toggled off once you're on-site, and motion alerts if you need eyes on a nearby unit between guests.

Also on IFTTT: more locks worth a look for hosts

A few more lock brands connect to IFTTT and are worth knowing about even though they did not make the main list above — either because they serve a narrower niche or because they are newer to most hosts' radar.

U-tec Ultraloq is a solid keyless option if you want fingerprint, code, key fob, or app-based entry all on one lock, with the option to share a digital key with guests directly. It is a reasonable alternative to Kwikset or Wyze if you want more unlock methods without jumping to Schlage or Yale pricing.

abode is not a lock on its own, but it is a common companion for hosts running a broader home-security setup alongside their lock — arming and disarming based on location, syncing with cameras, and centralizing alerts. Pair it with any of the locks above if you want one system watching the whole property, not just the door: U-tec Ultraloq + abode.

Selock is built specifically for the short-term rental and hospitality world, with remote lock control designed to plug into PMS, CRM, and booking systems. If you are running enough units that you want lock activity tied directly into your booking software rather than a general smart-home app, this is the more purpose-built option to research.

How to choose the right smart lock for your vacation rental

The right lock depends less on the brand and more on the hosting setup you are trying to run.

  • -If you host remotely and want the least friction possible, prioritize native Airbnb integration and dependable remote access.

  • -If you manage multiple properties, think about consistency. A lock that is easy to repeat across units usually matters more than squeezing every possible feature from one installation.

  • -If your property has restrictions, like HOA rules or historic hardware, work backward from what the building allows before comparing other features.

  • -If your internet is unreliable, do not pretend a Wi-Fi-first lock will become dependable just because the rest of the product page looks good.

  • -If you are on a tight budget, be honest about how hands-on you are willing to be. A cheaper lock can still be the right call, but only if you are not expecting enterprise-level convenience from it.

The bottom line

For most remote hosts, Yale Assure Lock 2 and Schlage Encode Plus are the strongest starting points. Schlage is the better pick if you want the safest all-around recommendation and care deeply about security. Yale is the better pick if you want a more flexible long-term setup, especially across multiple properties.

If your door cannot be changed, August is the workaround that keeps self-check-in on the table. If your Wi-Fi is unreliable, igloohome is the smarter choice. If you are testing the waters with a nearby unit and want to spend as little as possible, Wyze Lock Bolt can work as long as you accept the tradeoffs. And if none of the mainstream picks fit your setup, Ultraloq, abode, and Selock round out the IFTTT-connected options worth a second look.

The bigger opportunity, though, is not just choosing the right lock. It is choosing the right system around it. Once access is handled, IFTTT is what helps turn a lock into a smoother guest-arrival workflow — one that feels more automatic for you and more seamless for the person showing up at the door.

FAQs

Do these smart locks require a smart home hub?

Not usually. Schlage, Yale, August, Kwikset, and Wyze all connect to IFTTT directly through their own service, no separate hub needed. igloohome and Selock lean on PMS or app-based sync rather than a hub, and abode is itself a hub-style security layer you can pair with any of the locks above.

Which of these locks sync directly with Airbnb?

Schlage Encode Plus, Yale Assure Lock 2, and the August Wi-Fi Smart Lock all offer native Airbnb integration. Kwikset and Wyze do not, so guest codes on those need to be managed manually. igloohome and Selock connect through PMS sync rather than a direct native integration.

Does this work for VRBO or Booking.com listings too?

Yes. IFTTT automations trigger off the lock itself, not the booking platform, so everything here works the same regardless of where the reservation comes from. The one difference is native code generation: Schlage, Yale, and August's native integrations are built around Airbnb's reservation system specifically, so on VRBO or Booking.com you may need to generate and share guest codes manually unless the lock's own app supports those platforms directly.

What if my rental has unreliable Wi-Fi?

This is exactly the case igloohome is built for. It is designed to work reliably without depending on a strong internet connection, which makes it the better choice for cabins, rural properties, or anywhere signal drops are common.

Can I automate lighting and climate around any of these locks?

Yes. Every lock on this list can be paired with IFTTT Applets for guest-arrival lighting, thermostat prep, and host-side notifications, even the budget and offline-friendly options. The lock handles entry; IFTTT handles everything that happens around it.

Do I need a different lock for each property if I'm scaling to multiple units?

Not necessarily, but consistency helps. Yale Assure Lock 2 is the pick most hosts land on when standardizing across several properties, since it balances flexible guest-code handling with strong ecosystem support as a portfolio grows.
