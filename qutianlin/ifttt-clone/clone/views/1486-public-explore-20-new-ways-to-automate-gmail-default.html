20 new ways to automate Gmail on IFTTT

By The IFTTT Team

Published September 15, 2026

Updated September 16, 2026

If you've built Gmail Applets on IFTTT before, most of them probably ran one direction: something happened somewhere else, and Gmail sent an email about it. A form got submitted, a new row got added to a spreadsheet, a message landed in someone's inbox.

Those two send actions were the only thing IFTTT could do with Gmail. 20 new triggers, queries, and actions just joined them, bringing the Gmail service to 22 total: watching a conversation as it develops, adding or removing labels, saving a draft instead of sending it outright, pulling one specific attachment out of a thread by filename.

IFTTT flips the old one-way setup. A conversation, a label, or a draft sitting in your inbox can now be the trigger itself, reaching out to any of the 1,000+ other apps and devices Gmail already connects to.

Here's what's actually possible now, and the everyday problems it solves.

What's new in Gmail

Here's the full list:

Triggers: New email, New email matching search, New sent email, New conversation, New starred email, New attachment, New label

Queries: Get conversation, Find email, Get attachment by filename

Actions: Create a draft, Create a draft reply, Reply to email, Forward email, Add label to email, Remove label from email, Remove label from conversation, Create label, Archive email, Delete email

And here's what each of those actually does:

New email and New email matching search: One fires the moment any new message lands in your inbox, no conditions attached. The other only fires when a message matches a saved search. Pair it with almost any action and it covers most of what people actually want automated in Gmail.

New conversation and Get conversation: A trigger fires the moment a reply turns a single email into a back-and-forth, and a query can pull every message in that thread, in order, with every participant attached.

Create label, Add label to email, and Remove label from email or conversation: Labels can now be created, applied, and stripped by IFTTT itself, on one message or across a whole thread.

Create a draft and Create a draft reply: IFTTT writes the message and leaves it sitting in your Drafts folder. You still hit send.

New starred email and New attachment: One fires the first time you star a message, and the other fires whenever a new email arrives with an attachment. Both give you a foothold on emails you'd otherwise have to notice yourself.

New sent email and New label: One fires every time you send a message from Gmail, the other every time a new label gets created in your account.

Find email and Get attachment by filename: A query that returns the most recent messages matching a search on demand, and a query that returns one specific attachment from a message, located by its filename.

Reply to email, Forward email, Archive email, and Delete email round out the action list: sending a reply or forwarding a message outright, archiving it out of your inbox, or moving it to Trash.

Below are four ways people are already putting this to work, along with a quick-build link for each one so you can try it yourself.

1. Find what you need without scrolling for it

Searching Gmail by hand works fine until you're doing it every day for the same thing. New email matching search turns a saved search into a live trigger, Find email returns the most recent matches on demand, and Get attachment by filename goes straight to a specific file inside a message. From there it's a short trip to Google Docs, Google Drive, or Google Tasks, depending on what you're trying to keep.

5. Stop losing track of conversations that matter

A single email is easy to miss. A conversation that's been going back and forth for two days, with context buried three replies deep, is easy to lose entirely. The New conversation trigger fires the moment a thread actually becomes a conversation, and Get conversation can hand you every message in it, oldest to newest, so nothing gets left out of whatever you build next. Pair it with Slack for a heads-up in your team channel, Google Sheets for a running log, or Notion.so to archive the whole thread as a page.

6. Draft it now, decide later

Not every reply should go out the second it's written. Create a draft and Create a draft reply now leave the message waiting in your Drafts folder instead of sending it, so you keep the final say. Pair that with Reply to email or Forward email for the messages you're comfortable letting run all the way through, or trigger the draft off Google Calendar so a note is waiting before a meeting even starts.

Where to start

20 new triggers, queries, and actions is a lot to take in at once, so don't try to build all four types in one sitting. Pick whichever problem is actually costing you time this week: conversations you keep losing track of, an inbox you're tired of tidying by hand, replies you don't trust yourself to send unedited, or a search you're running manually for the tenth time. Build one Applet for that.
