Field Guide: Sports Coaches

Coaching a sports team requires your personal time both on and off the field — let Basecamp help you with the latter. 🙌🏼

In this guide, we’ll share examples and tips for using Basecamp to manage the administrative and communication chaos each season brings.

Getting started with your projects

Everything in Basecamp is organized into projects.

As a coach, you'll likely have a few types of projects — one shared with players and their families, one for staff or volunteers, and a private one for your own planning.

Working with your players and staff

Once your project is set up, you can invite players, parents, and staff to join.

Before sending invitations, take a moment to think about what level of permissions each person should have. You may want to keep some items private, limit who can create projects, or decide whether anyone needs admin-level access.

Basecamp has three user types: someone who works at your company, an outside collaborator, and a client. In this example, players and their families were added as clients, the assistant coach as someone who works at the company, and volunteers as outside collaborators. Here’s how that looks in the "Manage people" page:

Users are grouped by company. The first company is always your account’s main company, so you’ll see yourself and all the other folks who work at your company listed there. The coach also created separate companies for players and families, and for volunteers.

Players and families were added as clients because this user type has the most restricted access. This allows the coach to control exactly what they can see. For example, parents don’t need access to internal items like waiver tracking, so those remain private to the folks that work at the company and outside collaborators/volunteers.

Managing practices, games, and tasks

Running a team involves a lot of moving parts — practice planning, game-day prep, and everything in between. Basecamp helps you keep track of it all in one place.

The To-do tool will likely get more of a workout in the Staff and Volunteers project, where tasks can be created, assigned, and completed to keep the league running smoothly.

To-dos for staff and volunteers likely won’t change every season, so you'll definitely want to add those to your project template or create a To-do list template.

Schedules and game days

As things start moving, you’ll want a clear view of what’s happening and when.

The Calendar brings together everything with a date, including practices, games, meetings, and deadlines in a project.

Files and team resources

Keep documents, files, and links in one place, so they’re easy to find when you need them.

Use Docs & Files to store things like contact information, game rules, and other team materials.

If your team uses outside services like merch stores, registration forms, or shared drives, you can add them as External Links.

That way, your team can access everything from one place without digging through emails or bookmarks.

In the Teams project, two External Links were added — a team store for players and families, and a form and waiver tracker for the coach and staff:

Players and families will only see the ‘Team Store’ External Link when they access their project. This is because they were invited as clients and the "Family forms/waiver list" will be marked as 'private' so only the coach and other staff members in the project will see that External link.

💡 Tip: If an External Link leads to a service that requires a password, you'll want to get that information to your staff — you could even add it in the External Link's description!

You and your staff may want to keep important emails related to a project in Basecamp. To do that, turn on Email forwards and forward messages directly into your project.

Once added, everyone with access can read the email, reply, or leave comments for the rest of the team.

Staying on top of everything

Once everything is set up, you can stay on top of what’s happening without needing to check every project.

The My Bar gives you quick access to your tasks, upcoming events, bookmarks, and notes.

On the right, the notification sidebar shows recent activity across your projects, along with your Pings.

In addition to your notification settings, you’ll get a daily email with the latest activity and a weekly email with upcoming assignments. You can turn these off at any time.

Use Automatic Check-ins for recurring questions like “Are you available for the next game?” or “Any updates?”  to collect responses in one place.

That's a wrap for now! We hope this guide helps you save time so you can focus on getting your teams to the playoffs 🤞. And if you need any assistance with Basecamp, you can check out our learning center, help pages, or send us a note at Support. Happy Basecamping!
