How to Stay Productive Every Day with a Simple TickTick System

Some people seem to get a lot done every day. They finish their work on time, manage personal projects, maintain healthy habits, and still have time for hobbies and things they enjoy. It may look like they simply have stronger discipline. But in many cases, the real difference is not discipline. It is having a system that makes daily execution easier.

In this guide, we’ll build a simple Daily Productivity System with TickTick — from creating a realistic morning routine, planning weekly tasks, scheduling your day, breaking down complex projects, and keeping important tasks visible.

1. Build a Realistic Morning Routine System

A productive day often begins before work starts. Many people try to improve their mornings by adding more habits, but the biggest challenge is not creating a long routine. It is creating a routine that can actually be maintained.

An ideal morning routine may look impressive, but if it requires too much effort or does not fit your lifestyle, it will be difficult to follow consistently. A better approach is to build a simple system based on your actual schedule.

The purpose of a morning routine is not to complete as many tasks as possible. It is to create a stable starting point that helps your day begin smoothly.

Create a Dedicated Morning List in TickTick

A dedicated list can help separate morning activities from work tasks and other responsibilities. By keeping your morning routine in one place, you can clearly see the steps needed to start your day without mixing them with unrelated tasks.

For example, a Morning list may include recurring tasks such as:

These tasks can be set as recurring tasks, allowing the routine to appear automatically without needing to recreate it every day.

→ How to Set Up Recurring Tasks

💡 The most important principle is to design a routine that matches your real life. A simple routine that you can complete consistently is more valuable than a perfect routine that only works occasionally.

Use Multiple Reminders to Keep Your Routine on Track

For activities with a clear time range, reminders can help you transition between tasks without constantly checking the clock.

For example, if a morning activity is scheduled from 7:30 AM to 7:50 AM, using multiple reminders can make the process smoother:

  • A reminder before the activity starts helps you prepare.

  • A start reminder signals that it is time to begin.

  • An end reminder prevents one activity from taking too much time and affecting the rest of your schedule.

→ How to Set Up Task Reminders

This approach is useful because reminders do more than simply tell you when something happens. They act as signals that guide you through different stages of your day.

Instead of relying on memory or willpower, the system helps you move from one task to the next.

2. Plan Your Week and Turn It into Daily Actions

A weekly planning system helps create a bigger picture of your responsibilities and makes daily planning much easier. Instead of starting every day with an empty task list, you already have a prepared task pool. Each morning, you only need to decide which tasks deserve attention today.

Create a Workspace for Different Types of Tasks

To build this workflow in TickTick, start by creating a dedicated workspace for work-related tasks. The goal is to separate tasks based on their current status and priority, rather than putting everything into one large list.

For example, a work folder can include two main lists:

  • Weekly Plan

This list contains tasks that need to move forward during the current week. These are active tasks that require attention soon.

  • Someday

This list stores ideas and tasks that are worth keeping but do not require immediate action.

Keeping these tasks separate prevents future ideas from distracting you from current priorities while making sure important thoughts are not lost.

→ How to Create a Folder

Organize Weekly Tasks with Kanban View

The Weekly Plan list is where the weekly workflow happens. In the Weekly Plan list, set the grouping option to Group by Date and switch to Kanban view. Tasks will then be automatically organized into different columns based on their dates, such as:

  • Today

  • Tomorrow

  • No Date

→ How to Group Tasks by Date

→ How to Switch to Kanban View

This creates a simple visual workflow where you can quickly understand what needs attention now and what is still waiting to be scheduled.

The No Date group works as a flexible task pool. At the end of each week, review completed tasks and prepare the tasks you want to work on next week by adding them to the No Date group.

For tasks with clear due dates, you can assign dates in advance. Recurring tasks can also be set with dates and repeat rules, allowing regular responsibilities to automatically become part of your workflow.

For example:

  • Publish Instagram content every Tuesday

  • Review weekly data every Friday

  • Check social media every morning

Turn Weekly Plans into Daily Priorities

Each morning, review the tasks in No Date and move the tasks you want to focus on today into the Today group. Because the list is grouped by date, moving a task into a different date group will automatically update its date.

This makes daily planning faster and more flexible. You do not need to decide your entire week in advance. Instead, you can adjust your priorities based on your actual schedule and available time each day.

3. Turn Tasks into a Realistic Schedule with Calendar

A task list helps you decide what needs to be done, but it does not always show when the work will actually happen. After selecting your daily priorities, the next step is to turn those tasks into a realistic schedule.

In TickTick, you can view your tasks and calendar side by side on desktop. This makes it easy to see your available time and arrange tasks directly into your schedule.

→ How to Show Tasks and Calendar Together

By dragging tasks into the calendar, you can assign specific time blocks for each task and create a clearer plan for the day.

This also helps prevent over-planning. Instead of creating a long list of tasks without considering available time, you can immediately see whether your schedule is realistic.

Plan Time Based on Task Requirements

When scheduling tasks, the goal is not to fill every empty space on your calendar. It is to create a schedule that matches the way you work.

A few principles can help:

  • Schedule tasks that require more thinking or preparation earlier, especially when the process is unclear.

  • Protect longer blocks of uninterrupted time for important work.

  • Leave enough time before deadlines instead of waiting until the last moment.

  • Group smaller tasks together to reduce frequent task switching.

With these principles, your calendar becomes more than a place to record events. It becomes a tool for turning your task list into an actionable daily plan.

4. Make Complex Tasks Easier to Start

Planning tells you what to do, but execution becomes difficult when a task feels too large or unclear. For complex tasks, the biggest challenge is often not the amount of work itself, but not knowing where to begin.

Before starting, break the task down into smaller actions. Think through:

  • What should I do first?

  • What comes next?

  • Are there any important things I need to pay attention to?

Then add these steps as subtasks inside TickTick.

→ How to Create Subtasks

Smaller steps make the task feel easier to start, reduce the chance of getting stuck, and create continuous progress through small wins.

Track Progress So You Can Easily Continue Tasks

Some tasks cannot be completed in one day. They may take several days or even weeks to finish, with weekends, holidays, or other priorities in between. When returning to a task after some time, it is easy to forget what has already been done, what information was collected, or what should happen next.

To avoid starting over every time, keep important progress information directly inside the task details. You can record:

  • Completed steps

  • Important notes or links

  • Current progress

  • The next action to take

→ How to Use Markdown in Task Details

This creates a simple progress record and makes it easier to continue from where you stopped.

For tasks that require continuous updates while working, TickTick also allows you to open a task as a Sticky Note. The Sticky Note can stay floating above other windows, allowing you to view the task details, update progress, or capture new ideas while working without repeatedly switching back to TickTick.

By keeping the task and progress information visible, you can stay focused on the current work and continue moving forward more easily.

→ How to Open a Task as a Sticky Note

5. Keep Your Productivity System Visible on Your Home Screen

A productivity system works best when the right information is easy to access. Even with a clear plan, it is easy to lose track of tasks when you need to repeatedly open the app and search for what to do next.

To make the system easier to follow, place different parts of your daily workflow directly on your phone home screen.

For example:

  • Morning Routine — tasks to complete before starting work

  • Today Work — all work-related tasks planned for today

  • Evening — personal and household tasks after work

With these widgets always visible, you can quickly check what needs to be done at different times of the day without repeatedly opening TickTick.

Create Filters and Display Them with Widgets

These home screen sections are created by combining TickTick filters and widgets.

First, create filters to show the tasks you need at different moments of the day.

For example:

  • Morning Routine: show today’s tasks from the Morning list

  • Today Work: show today’s work tasks

  • Evening: show personal tasks planned for today

→ How to Create a Custom Filter

Then, add TickTick task widgets to your phone home screen and set each widget to display the corresponding filter.

→ How to Add TickTick Widgets to Your Home Screen

Conclusion

A productive day does not come from simply adding more tasks to a to-do list. The real difference comes from building a system that makes it easier to decide what to do, when to do it, and how to keep making progress.

With TickTick, you can connect different parts of your workflow together:

  • A realistic morning routine helps you start the day smoothly.

  • Weekly planning helps you prepare tasks before they become urgent.

  • Calendar scheduling turns plans into actionable time blocks.

  • Task breakdown and progress tracking make complex work easier to continue.

  • Filters and widgets keep important information visible throughout the day.

Everyone has a different way of working, so there is no single productivity system that fits everyone perfectly. The goal is not to copy someone else’s workflow, but to build a system that matches your own needs and helps you work with less friction.

If this workflow gives you some ideas, try adapting it to create your own Daily Productivity System with TickTick.
